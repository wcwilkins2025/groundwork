A place of your own. From wherever you are.

Most of us will never have half a million dollars to buy a ranch. That is not the way in. The way in is a path, one stage at a time, and it starts from where you stand today.

Maybe you have a job you want out of. Maybe you have no job. Maybe you have debt, a family, and no idea what a cow costs. Every man who ever made it to his own place started somewhere, and a lot of them started with nothing but a willingness to work for another man first.

Groundwork lays out that path for you. Not a dream and not a lecture. Six stages, each with a gate you can see, what to do this month, and what it costs, with every number pulled from a public source and shown to you. Where there is no published figure, it says so.

It will tell you the truth. If the numbers say eight years, it says eight years. If they say your health insurance is the thing in the way, it says that. A man can work with the truth. He cannot work with a number that closes the page.

How it works

  1. 1Say where you are. Ten plain questions: cash, debt, money coming in, who is coming with you, whether you have worked cattle. Skip what you do not know.
  2. 2See your path. Six stages from where you stand to living off a place. Which one you are in, the gate to the next, what to do this month, how long each one takes on your numbers.
  3. 3Run your numbers. What a place costs to set up and run in your county, and what it takes to live off it with cattle on leased grass. Change anything.
  4. 4Keep it. Your plan rides in the link. Bookmark it, print it, paste it in a post. If you want it on every phone and computer, sign in with your email and it is kept for you, one row that only you can read, until you delete it. What is kept and what is not.

The six stages

  1. Stage

    Get steady

    Money coming in covers money going out, and there is a cushion in the bank. Income first: ranch hand, fence crew, custom haying, sale barn. A year on another man's place is the schooling, and it is paid.

  2. Stage

    Prove yourself

    Two years working cattle for someone else. It is what the loan programs count as experience, and it is where the leased grass and the cows on shares come from. Nobody leases to a stranger.

  3. Stage

    First cattle without capital

    Caring for other men's pairs, or cows on shares, on leased grass, while the job continues. Panels and a chute. A government microloan buys the first cows if you want them.

  4. Stage

    Grow

    Keep the heifers and the herd grows about a third a year. Add leased acres. Own more of the herd.

  5. Stage

    Land

    A place of your own, bought with the government farm ownership programs that lend forty years and take five percent down from a man with three years of experience. Buy one with the house, well and septic already there.

  6. Stage

    Live off it

    The place, the cattle and what you keep from work cover the family. The numbers show what that takes in your county.
