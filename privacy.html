What is kept

In plain words. This is a promise, not a legal document.

If you never sign in

Nothing is kept. Your answers ride in the web address of the page. They are not sent to us and no one can see them unless you give them the link. Close the page and they are gone unless you bookmarked it.

If you sign in

We keep your email address, and the answers in your plan when you press Save. That is the numbers and choices from Where you are and the debt list from Get steady. We do not ask for your name, your address, your account numbers, your documents or your location, and we do not want them.

Your plan is one row in a database, and the database is set so that only you, signed in, can read or change your row. There is no report, no list, no sharing and no selling. No one reads the rows.

Deleting it

On Your account, press Delete my account. Your sign-in and your row go that day, for good. The link you have still works on its own.

Email

Your email is used to send you the sign-in link and, if you turn them on, the check-ins. Nothing else. You can turn check-ins off at any time, on Your account or from the link at the bottom of any check-in.

If you turn check-ins on, we also keep how often you want them, when the last one went out, and your answers to each one. Your answers can change your plan: days of cattle work you report count toward stage 2, and numbers you type replace the ones in your plan. Each check-in email carries a private link that works for 45 days. Anyone with that link can answer for you, so do not forward the email.

The check-ins are automated. They are filled in from your plan and the published figures. No one reads them or your answers.

If Groundwork ever shuts down

Every signed-in man gets his plan sent to him as a file first.

Where it lives

The rows are kept with a hosted database service in the United States, under an account that belongs to the man who runs Groundwork and to no company. The page itself is served from GitHub Pages. Check-in emails are sent once a day by a scheduled job on GitHub, through an email delivery service, Resend; they see your email address and the check-in, and nothing else. The code is open and anyone can read how it works.

Questions or a dead link: say so where you found Groundwork and it gets fixed.
